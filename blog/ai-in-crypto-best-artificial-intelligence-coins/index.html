What Is AI in Crypto?

So, what is crypto AI? In a nutshell, AI in crypto implies using artificial intelligence, including machine learning, neural networks, generative AI, etc., in blockchain solutions. Artificial intelligence most often forms the basis of smart contracts to make them more intelligent and adaptable to the specific use context. In contrast, the conventional approach implies using a strict algorithm that operates according to pre-defined conditions and cannot independently think, compare, or learn.

Why AI and Crypto Are a Powerful Combination

AI and cryptocurrency complement each other. These two concepts can be effective in:

  • Automating complex decisions. Artificial intelligence enables automating both regular scripts and more complex scenarios. In DeFi, to reduce fees or minimize the risks of low volatility, developers can use AI to determine when it's best to move assets between pools.
  • Big data analysis. Blockchains store colossal amounts of raw data that are difficult to analyze using traditional algorithms. Here, AI can be useful for predicting price fluctuations, determining the whales' behavior, analyzing market sentiment, etc.
  • Adaptive smart contracts. AI-based smart contracts become dynamic and gain the ability to change their logic depending on user behavior or the market situation. This is important for future DAOs, decentralized marketplaces, and AI assistants.

How to Evaluate AI Crypto Coins

Let's abstract from the above-described AI crypto projects and try to understand how to "peel off the husk" from the solution you are interested in.

  • Team and expertise. First, you need to find out who is its CEO and whether he or she have dealt with projects that have already passed the MVP stage. It will be great if they also have an academic background in artificial intelligence and regularly participate in conferences for developers and scientists.
  • Whitepaper. The top cryptocurrency AI project's architecture should be transparent and understandable, i.e., in the whitepaper, the team must describe the AI stack. It is also worth looking in the whitepaper for details on model training to find out which data sets are used, how often fine-tuning is performed, and how protection against data poisoning is implemented.
  • Use cases. Now, you have to find out whether the project solves a specific problem – for example, it could be optimization of DeFi strategies, automation of KYC, generation of models for DAO, or something else. Also, be sure to check whether the MVP/demo version/access to the API or SDK is ready.
  • Community and open sourceness. Analyze the project team's activity on GitHub, namely the frequency of commits, the number of contributors, open issues, etc. Additionally, evaluate the community's involvement in Discord, Telegram, and X (Twitter) to understand whether the technical details of the project are discussed there (and not just ordinary chatter). It will be an advantage if the team publishes news about receiving grants from large networks such as Polygon, Solana, Arbitrum, etc.
  • Tokenomics and economic model. Find information on why the project token is needed. In reliable ones, it takes part in staking or, for example, provides access to AI models or DAO management. In addition, be sure to find out how tokens are distributed and whether there are appropriate protection mechanisms.
  • Risks and vulnerabilities. Clarify how the project validates training data and whether there is protection against attacks. It will be great if the project has passed a technical audit performed by a third party. Also, don't forget to assess the legal risks associated with the non-compliance with GDPR and/or CCPA standards.

In general, always follow the DYOR principle, and then the risks of investing in a "bubble" project will be minimal.

The Future of AI in the Crypto Space

The merger of AI and Web3 opens up new horizons for the following directions of the crypto space:

  • DAO. With the help of AI, DAOs will be able to guide management decisions made by AI agents trained on community activity and on-chain data. In particular, AI can analyze proposals in the DAO and rank them by importance, or automatically reject spam.
  • Portfolio management. Artificial intelligence can automate the work of asset managers to diversify the portfolio, optimize the balance, and implement hedging. Unlike classic DeFi protocols, such agents can take into account external macroeconomic data and events in real time.
  • Code review and vulnerability screening. Neural networks can be useful in auditing smart contracts to detect vulnerabilities and other imperfections in the code. AI can also independently rank projects based on investment prospects.

Today, many funds, including Coinbase, a16z, and Binance Labs, invest in the deployment of AI Web3 infrastructures. Therefore, with the right idea positioning, some AI crypto projects will receive a good chance of gaining strong support.
